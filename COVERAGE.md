## Claims requiring careful interpretation

The audit distinguishes vocal-only language definitions from natural signed languages; arbitrary from random; additive from balanced bilingualism; acquisition sequence from receptive ability; current dominance from first acquisition; multilingual difference from disorder; home-language support from a blanket language ban; vocabulary teaching from concept/structure teaching; and measured transfer from assumed transfer.

Acquisition stages, vocabulary counts and timing figures are teaching approximations, not universal diagnostic cutoffs. Cummins’s models are conceptual accounts rather than literal neuroanatomy. Cognitive-benefit claims must match measured outcomes and study limitations. Infant tuning and word-combination expectations are stated cautiously. Cultural, gender and class stereotypes are not taught as universal laws. Negative concord and variable copula absence are described as constrained systems. Regional labels, boundaries and historical priority claims remain source-sensitive. The unverified public-figure quotation is not reused as a verified quote.
