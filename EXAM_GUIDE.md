# Study and exam preparation

Begin each module with its essay. Explain the central distinctions aloud before looking at the glossary model. In a short answer, define the concept, identify the evidence in the example and state an important distinction or limitation. The ten written-answer models demonstrate possible answers rather than an official marking scheme.

Use instant-feedback practice to learn. After a wrong answer, identify why the option seemed plausible and which stated fact favors the key. Explain the alternatives rather than memorizing the answer letter: positions are shuffled. Later return to wrong-answer revision without relying on the earlier display order.

Use a mock exam after studying the modules. Start with 25 questions, then use 50 or 100 for broader practice. The pool is finite, so later tests may repeat cases. Review the explanation even after a correct answer if your reasoning was uncertain. A high repeated-test score does not independently establish mastery of unseen material.

For bilingual profiles, separate acquisition history, current proficiency, domain, modality and support. For clinical cases, distinguish a plausible explanation from an established diagnosis and choose the next step supported by evidence. For BICS/CALP/CUP, describe the actual task demands and transfer conditions. For culture, ask about the person instead of assuming a national rule. For dialect, identify the observed linguistic level and separate social status from grammatical capacity.

When a slide uses a simplified or contested claim, learn its course term while giving the careful explanation provided in the audit. Exact word counts, fixed ages, oldest-dialect claims, universal gender patterns and broad cognitive-superiority claims should not be turned into unsupported facts. If your instructor specifies a different terminology convention, identify it explicitly rather than silently mixing definitions.

Use the 103-slide map to check that you have visited each substantive topic. Export progress before moving devices or clearing browser data. Course backup and a mock-exam draft are separate browser-local records.
